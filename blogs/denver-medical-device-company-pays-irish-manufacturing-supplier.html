How a Denver Medical Device Company Pays an Irish Manufacturing Supplier

A Denver-based medical device company sources a contract-manufactured part from an Irish supplier every quarter. Here is how the accounts payable team gets that invoice paid correctly, the first time.

A Denver-based medical device company that sources a molded component or a contract-manufactured subassembly from an Irish supplier faces a different problem than a freelancer sending money home: this is a recurring, invoice-driven B2B payment, not a personal transfer, and it needs to reconcile against a purchase order every time.

Multiple large medtech and pharma companies run production and contract-manufacturing operations there, which means US device makers of every size, not just the giants, often end up sourcing a component, a subassembly, or a finished device from an Irish supplier. If Cork, Galway, or Dublin just showed up on your Denver company's accounts payable desk for the first time, here's what changes.

Why Ireland Matters for Medical Device and Pharma Manufacturing Sourcing

Ireland built its manufacturing base around life sciences over several decades. Multinational medtech and pharma manufacturers set up plants there, and over time, a deep network of contract manufacturers, component suppliers, and specialized parts makers grew up around them. For a small or mid-size US medical device company, this means a supplier in Ireland may be the most practical source for a specific molded part, a sterile packaging component, or a subassembly that meets the regulatory standards your device needs.

Once your Denver engineering or sourcing team signs a supply agreement with an Irish vendor, your accounting team needs a reliable way to pay that vendor's invoices on time, every cycle, without the payment getting held up over a bank-detail mismatch.

What You Need Before You Pay an Irish Supplier

Ireland is part of the SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area) system, so payments there rely on a few standard identifiers. Get those four right and the payment reconciles without a phone call; miss one and it can sit unmatched while a shipment waits on it.

Supplier IBAN

The International Bank Account Number identifies the exact account your payment lands in. Confirm it directly with your supplier's finance contact rather than reusing details from an older invoice.

Bank BIC or SWIFT code

This identifies the receiving bank itself. An international payment to Ireland typically needs both the IBAN and the BIC to route correctly.

Invoice or purchase order reference

Include the invoice number or PO reference so the supplier's accounts receivable team can match the incoming payment to the correct invoice in their system.

Business purpose code

A short description like "goods purchase" or "manufacturing services" tells both banks why the money is moving.

Keeping Your Vendor Payment Records Audit-Ready

A medical device company answers to auditors, to its own quality system, and sometimes to regulators, so payment records need to hold up to scrutiny. Save a copy of the invoice, the purchase order, and the payment confirmation together for every supplier transaction, with the invoice reference visible on the payment record itself, not just in a separate email thread.

If your company works with multiple Irish suppliers, keep a simple log of IBANs and BICs on file so your accounts payable team is not re-collecting the same details every payment cycle. This also makes it easier to catch a typo or an outdated bank detail before a payment goes out.

Common Mistakes That Delay Supplier Payments to Ireland

The most common delay comes from a mismatched or outdated IBAN, often because a supplier changed banks and did not update every customer. The second most common issue is a missing or vague invoice reference, which leaves the supplier's team guessing which shipment or purchase order the payment covers. Confirming supplier bank details directly with your contact before the first payment, and keeping that information current, solves most of these problems before they start.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an IBAN and a BIC?

An IBAN identifies the specific bank account your supplier uses, while a BIC (also called a SWIFT code) identifies the bank itself. A payment to Ireland typically needs both.

How does a US medical device company find a supplier's correct bank details?

Ask your supplier's finance contact directly, ideally by phone or a verified email address, rather than relying on details printed on an older invoice, since banking information can change.

Why does the invoice reference matter so much?

Without a clear invoice reference, your supplier's accounts receivable team has to guess which shipment or purchase order your payment covers, which can delay confirmation and even hold up your next shipment.

Can a small or mid-size medical device company use the same payment process as a large manufacturer?

Yes. The underlying process is the same for a small supplier relationship as it is for a large one. What changes is volume, not the steps of confirming bank details, referencing the invoice, and keeping records.

What records should accounts payable keep for an Ireland supplier payment?

Keep the original invoice, the matching purchase order, and the payment confirmation together, with the invoice reference visible on all three, so the transaction is easy to trace later.

Conclusion

Sourcing a component from an Irish manufacturer is common for US medical device companies, and paying that supplier correctly comes down to a few repeatable habits: verified bank details, a clear invoice reference, and organized records. Once your Denver accounts payable team has a process in place, each new Ireland invoice becomes a routine task instead of a scramble.

A healthcare staffing agency in San Diego placing Filipino-American nurses in local hospitals and clinics runs into the Philippines corridor in two completely different ways. One nurse asks for help sending support to a parent back home on a recurring basis. Separately, the agency itself owes an invoice to a Philippine-based credentialing or recruiting partner who verifies license and background documentation before a placement goes through. Same destination country, two different purposes, two different sets of records.

Treating both the same way, out of the same running total, is where a small agency's books get messy. This guide keeps them apart from the start.

Key Insight: A family support payment and a vendor payment to the same country should never share a line in your records. One is a personal favor your agency is helping facilitate for an employee; the other is a business expense tied to an invoice. Label each recipient clearly as one or the other the first time you save them, and the separation holds automatically on every payment after that.

Two Flows, Not One: Family Support and a Vendor Invoice

The temptation is to treat every Philippines payment as one bucket. It shouldn't be. A family support payment is personal money moving on behalf of an employee, usually the same amount to the same person on a recurring schedule. A credentialing partner's invoice is a business expense tied to a specific verification job, with its own invoice number and its own due date.

Setting Up a Recurring Family Support Payment

This is the employee's own money, sent from the employee's own Zil Remit account. The agency's role is limited to pointing staff to the service and helping with setup, not funding the transfer. That distinction is what keeps it a personal payment instead of employee compensation the agency would need to run through payroll. A nurse employee's family support payment is usually the same person, the same rough amount, and the same cadence every time. Set it up once and it becomes routine instead of a fresh decision every month.

Save the recipient's bank and account details once

Get the recipient's full legal name, bank, and account number directly from the employee, matching the name on the receiving account exactly, and save it as a template for future payments.

Check in on the details periodically

Set a reminder, quarterly is reasonable, to confirm with the family member that the account is still current before the next scheduled payment goes out.

Check the exchange rate before each send

Label it clearly as personal, not business

Keep this payment out of the agency's vendor or accounts payable records. It's the employee's own money moving through their own account, not an agency-funded payment, so it isn't a business expense. If the agency ever funds a transfer like this directly instead of the employee, treat it as compensation and route it through payroll, not around it.

Paying a Philippine Credentialing or Recruiting Partner

A staffing agency working with a Philippine-based credentialing service or recruiting partner is paying a real vendor invoice, not a personal transfer. Before the first payment goes out, confirm:

Confirm Why it matters
Legal business name on the accountShould match the name on the signed services agreement, not a similarly named entity
Bank and account numberPulled directly from the invoice or agreement, not a chat message or a changed email
Invoice numberUsed as the payment reference, so the vendor's own accounting can match it automatically
Scope of the serviceTies the payment to a specific placement or verification batch for your own records

A foreign vendor payment also has a documentation step a domestic vendor doesn't: collect a Form W-8BEN-E (or W-8BEN for an individual) from the partner before the first payment, so there's a record supporting the vendor's foreign status on file. Ask your accountant how this specific engagement should be treated; this isn't tax advice.

Treat any change to a vendor's previously confirmed bank details as suspicious by default, even from an established partner, and confirm it by phone using a number from your existing records before sending the next payment.

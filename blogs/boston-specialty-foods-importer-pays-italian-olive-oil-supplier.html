How a Boston Specialty Foods Importer Pays an Italian Olive Oil Supplier

A practical walkthrough of paying an Italian supplier: the IBAN and BIC fields that matter, the payment reference Italian producers expect, and a step-by-step process built around a harvest-season order.

ZR

Sabeer Nelli, CEO of Zil Money

Zil Remit

A Boston specialty foods importer buying extra-virgin olive oil from a family cooperative in Puglia works on the cooperative's calendar, not their own. The oil is pressed between October and December, the invoice is due on shipment, and the supplier expects payment the way an Italian bank expects to see it: a full IBAN, a matching business name, and a reference line tied to the invoice.

Key Insight: Two of the most common causes of delay when paying an Italian supplier are a beneficiary name that doesn't match their business registration, and a missing payment reference the supplier needs for their own bookkeeping.

Reading an Italian IBAN and BIC

An Italian IBAN is 27 characters and carries more information than a US account number. Knowing what each part means makes it easier to catch a transposition error before it delays payment.

Component Format What it means
Country code2 lettersIT for Italy
Check digits2 digitsValidates the IBAN checksum
CIN1 letterInternal control character
ABI code5 digitsIdentifies the supplier's bank
CAB code5 digitsIdentifies the bank branch
Account number12 charactersThe supplier's own account

The BIC (or SWIFT code) follows a separate pattern: a bank code, country code, and location code, sometimes with a branch code. If a supplier's invoice still shows an older domestic-format account number instead of the full IBAN, ask for the current IBAN before sending. The two formats aren't interchangeable.

Build in a buffer before the harvest deadline. A US-to-Italy business payment routes through the international banking network rather than a same-day domestic transfer, and Italian banks slow down or close around national holidays, including the mid-August Ferragosto period. Ask whether fees are covered so the full invoice amount reaches the supplier, rather than a reduced amount after fees are deducted along the way.

Paying the Puglia cooperative, step by step

1

Match the beneficiary exactly to the cooperative's registered business name, not the brand name on the label — small producers often differ between the two.

2

Check the ABI and CAB codes against the bank name the supplier gave you, not just that the field is filled in.

Italian suppliers use this reference line to match incoming payments to their own invoices and records. Match it to the invoice number so the payment is easy for them to reconcile on their end.

Mistakes that delay a supplier payment

  • Paying the personal IBAN of whoever signed the invoice instead of the cooperative's business account, a mistake common with small family producers who mix personal and business banking.
  • Using an outdated account number format instead of the full 27-character IBAN from an older invoice template.
  • Leaving out the causale reference, which can delay the supplier's own reconciliation and, in turn, their confirmation that the shipment can move.
  • Waiting until the last day of the harvest window to pay, leaving no buffer if a detail needs correcting.

Getting the payment right the first time

A B2B payment to an Italian supplier isn't complicated once the IBAN, the beneficiary name, and the causale line up. Delay usually comes down to one of those three details being off, not the payment method itself. Building a short pre-payment check into your process, confirm the beneficiary name, validate the full IBAN, add the reference, catches the mistake before it costs a shipment.
